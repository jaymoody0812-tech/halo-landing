Why this exists.

HALO started as a mood tracker for one person trying to stay functional long enough to figure out what was wrong. Over two years and 27,000 conversation turns, it became a research method, a clinical data source, and a platform.

The Cascade Compression Theory (CCT) — the framework underneath HALO — predicts when people go silent before they deteriorate. The data says 47% of the time they do. Standard intake tools catch almost none of it.

Built in Central New York. Validated on real data. No VC funding, no team, no IRB yet — just a method that works and a case study that says so.
