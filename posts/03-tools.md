# Three tools I built. All free. Here's why.

*Posted to Substack — HALO System by Jacob Moody*

---

People keep asking me when I'm going to charge for this and I understand why they're asking but the answer for right now is: I'm not. Here's why and also here's what the tools actually are because I realize I've been talking about HALO for a while without explaining what it does in plain language.

**The caregiver app.** 

This is the one I'm most proud of and also the one I built for the most personal reason. My wife and I spent the last few years dealing with a lot of the same stuff that unpaid caregivers deal with — mental health crises, a newborn, financial pressure, no vehicle, the whole thing — and I noticed there's basically no tool for the person *doing the caregiving.* Everything is built for the patient. The caregiver is supposed to just... absorb it. So I built something for them. It does mood tracking, weekly check-ins, crisis resource routing, a chat that remembers your history. It runs on Render and Supabase, it's a real app with real data storage, and it's free because the people who need it most are usually the ones who can't afford another subscription.

Try it: https://halo-caregiver-app.onrender.com

**The threat analysis dashboard.**

This one's harder to explain quickly so I'll just say it's a cascade prediction system — it ingests live news feeds, cross-references them against historical pattern data and peer-reviewed literature, and flags when a geopolitical or social situation shows the early signatures of the compression pattern I've been studying. It's the research side of HALO, the thing I use to validate that Cascade Compression Theory applies beyond mental health. The same pattern — systems under load going quiet before they give — shows up in political regimes, economic indicators, epidemics. I have the sim data.

Try it: https://halo-ta-cct.onrender.com

**The tutor.**

This one is exactly what it sounds like — an academic companion I built for myself when I was transferring to Oswego, grounded in real sources, not just vibes. It pulls from PubMed, peer-reviewed lit, and the Seshat Databank (which is coded world history data, not a buzzword). It's built to help with social science coursework specifically, not to do the work for you. It teaches the method.

Try it: https://halo-tutor-1.onrender.com

---

Here's the honest answer on the "why free" question. It's free because I'm still building toward the real pitch which is licensing this to CNY institutions — county mental health orgs, SUNY, hospitals — and if I start charging individuals before I've done that, I've built a subscription app instead of a platform. Those are different things. The caregiver app is meant to reach the people who can't get to a therapist, not compete with one. The TA is meant to be a research tool that ends up in a lab, not behind a paywall.

If it matters to you and you want to chip in on the build costs — servers, API credits, the time — there's a PayPal link at the bottom. I'm not going to make a big thing of it. The work stands or it doesn't.

What I do need is people actually using these. If you're a caregiver, or you know one, or you're in CNY mental health research, or you're at SUNY — those are the people I built this for. Share it with someone who needs it.
